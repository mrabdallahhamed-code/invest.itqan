A Foreign Company’s Obligations After Incorporation in Saudi Arabia: A Practical Follow-Up List

Last updated: June 2026 · Itqan Business Services team

Incorporation is not the end of the road. After start-up, periodic obligations arise towards several authorities, and failing to meet them costs penalties and may disrupt your business. Here are the most important ones in a follow-up list that helps you miss nothing.

⚠️ Regulations, conditions and requirements are constantly being renewed and may change after this article’s publication date. What appears here is general information, not legal advice, so we confirm the current requirements for your activity before any submission.

Why Talk About What Comes After Incorporation?

The costly mistakes usually come after incorporation, when the owner is busy with operations and sales and misses a renewal or a return. And because the authorities involved are many, it is hard to remember everything without a follow-up system. That is why we advise preparing an obligations schedule from the first month.

1. The Annual Update of the Investment Registration

The Investment Law and its regulations require registration details to be updated annually, and any change in ownership, activity or contributions to be documented within the set period.

2. Commercial Registration and Licences

Follow the renewal of the commercial registration and the updating of its details, renew municipal licences and activity licences before they expire, and update the national address when you move.

3. Zakat, Income Tax and VAT

Returns and payments are submitted on time to the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority, with compliance with e-invoicing requirements. What applies to your company depends on its ownership structure and activity: foreign partners’ shares are treated differently from Saudi and GCC partners’ shares. It is therefore important to study the structure before incorporation and then monitor it after start-up.

4. Social Insurance and Wages

Register employees with social insurance, pay contributions monthly, and follow wage protection through the Ministry of Human Resources platforms.

5. Saudization and Hiring Talent

Establishments are subject to nationalisation programmes according to activity and size, so plan your hiring from the start so you meet the required percentages.

6. Books and Financial Statements

Keep regular books and prepare financial statements under the approved standards, and keep documents for the statutory period; this is the basis of any review or future financing.

7. Residencies and Work Permits

Follow the validity of expatriate workers’ residencies and work permits and renew them before they expire, and complete procedures for leavers.

8. Keeping the Registered Foreign Company Active

The foreign company is the registered investor, so make sure it remains active and in good standing in its home country and renew whatever is needed of its records. For details read why you should not dissolve your foreign company after registration.

A Suggested Follow-Up Schedule

  • Monthly: insurance, wages and invoices.
  • Quarterly: VAT returns according to the period set for your establishment.
  • Annually: the annual update of the investment registration, the zakat or tax return, renewal of the register and licences, and the financial statements.

Note

This list is illustrative and not exhaustive; obligations vary with your activity and the size of your establishment, and their dates are set by the competent authorities.
