Investment Registration

Your Complete Guide to Investment Registration (MISA License) in Saudi Arabia

Last updated: September 2026 · Itqan Business Services team

If you are planning to set up a company in Saudi Arabia as a foreign investor, your first regulatory stop is investment registration with the Ministry of Investment, widely known as the “MISA license”.

What Has Changed?

The updated Investment Law took effect in February 2025 and replaced the previous foreign investment regime. Since then the official procedure is called “investment registration”, and the investor receives an investment registration certificate. The term “MISA license” is still commonly used to refer to the same procedure.

What Is Investment Registration?

It is the procedure through which the Ministry of Investment allows a foreign investor to own or take part in a company inside the Kingdom. It is a preliminary step rather than a permit to operate in itself — it is followed by commercial registration and sector licenses before operations begin.

Who Needs It?

  • Foreign investors, individuals or companies, who want to set up a wholly owned company
  • Mixed (Saudi–foreign) partnerships
  • Branches of foreign companies that wish to operate in the Kingdom
  • Investors who wish to acquire a stake in an existing Saudi company

GCC nationals are exempt from this procedure and register directly with the Ministry of Commerce.

Documents Usually Required

  • Articles of association and commercial registration of the parent company, notarised and attested
  • Audited financial statements for the last fiscal year
  • Passport copy of the company’s representative
  • Details of partners and authorised signatories
  • A letter of intent or brief study of the intended activity

Documents vary by activity and legal form; review the negative list of restricted activities before applying.

Expected Timeline

When the file is complete, investment registration typically takes 5 to 15 working days, followed by the time needed to issue the articles of association and commercial registration. The biggest cause of delay is missing documents.

Next Steps After Registration

  1. Complete incorporation and obtain the commercial registration from the Ministry of Commerce
  2. Obtain any municipal, professional or industrial licenses required for the activity
  3. Open a corporate bank account
  4. Register with social insurance (GOSI) and the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority
