Steps to Set Up a 100% Foreign-Owned Company in Saudi Arabia: From Investment Registration to Start-Up

Last updated: June 2026 · Itqan Business Services team

The route to incorporation is clear once you know its order: an existing foreign company whose documents are authenticated, then investment registration in its name with the Ministry of Investment, then incorporating the Saudi company and completing its registrations. Here are the steps in their usual order and what to watch at each one.

⚠️ Regulations, conditions and requirements are constantly being renewed and may change after this article’s publication date. What appears here is general information, not legal advice, so we confirm the current requirements for your activity before any submission.

Why the Order Matters

Many investors think incorporation is a single step. In fact it is a chain: each stage depends on the output of the one before. If the activity description in the investment registration differs from the one in the incorporation contract, or a company name differs between documents, the process goes backwards and time is lost. That is why we recommend planning the whole route before submitting the first application.

First Requirement: Own an Existing, Qualified Foreign Company

Investment registration is currently made in the name of an existing foreign company, not in the name of an individual investor. You therefore first need a foreign company with a commercial registration and financial statements for its last fiscal year, and it must be qualified under the investment registration conditions.

If you do not own a foreign company that fits this description, we acquire an existing, qualified foreign company for you in line with the investment registration conditions. See also why you should not dissolve your foreign company after registration.

Before You Start: Define Your Activity in Saudi Arabia

The activity you will carry out in Saudi Arabia is not necessarily the activity of your foreign parent company; most investors choose an activity different from their original one. What matters is that the activity is open to foreign investment and is registered under the correct type of registration. Mixing a commercial activity with a service registration, for example, delays the application. See the difference between service and commercial licences.

Step 1: Authenticate the Foreign Company’s Documents

The foreign company needs its commercial registration and its financial statements for the last fiscal year, authenticated either by the Saudi embassy in the country of issue or by Apostille in countries where it is available. This stage depends on bodies outside the Kingdom, so start it early.

Step 2: Investment Registration in the Foreign Company’s Name

The application is submitted to the Ministry of Investment in the foreign company’s name, stating the intended activity, the partners’ details and the ownership structure. Once accepted, the details are entered in the national investor register and the investment registration certificate is issued.

Step 3: Incorporate the Saudi Company

We recommend that the Saudi company be a limited liability company: it is the most suitable entity for most foreign investors because it limits the partners’ liability and allows flexible management. The incorporation contract is then prepared, stating the company name, purpose, capital, partners’ shares and managers’ powers, and it is usually signed through approved digital channels.

A common mistake is copying a ready-made contract that fits neither your activity nor your partners, which causes problems later when a new partner joins or financing is sought. Precise drafting at the start saves you costly amendments.

Step 4: Commercial Registration

Once the contract is approved, the company is entered in the commercial register at the Ministry of Commerce, receives its commercial registration number and becomes an existing legal person.

Step 5: Statutory Registrations After Commercial Registration

After registration, the company’s registrations with the competent authorities are completed, mainly:

  • The Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority: zakat or income tax depending on the ownership structure, and VAT where applicable.
  • The General Organization for Social Insurance when employing staff.
  • Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development platforms (such as Qiwa) and Passports services (Muqeem) for managing expatriate workers.
  • Municipal licences and activity-specific licences, if your activity requires them.
  • The establishment’s national address.

Not every activity needs all of these registrations; an establishment that does not employ staff at the beginning, for example, will not need employment files immediately.

Step 6: Bank Account and Start-Up

Once registrations are complete you open the company’s bank account, start operating and issue invoices in line with e-invoicing requirements.

How Long Do These Steps Take?

Time varies with the activity, the completeness of documents and the authorities’ responses. The investment registration stage usually takes 5 to 15 working days, not including the time needed to authenticate documents outside the Kingdom. What delays projects most is missing or inconsistent documents, not the procedures themselves.
