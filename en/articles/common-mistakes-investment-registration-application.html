Common Mistakes in Investment Registration Applications and How to Avoid Them

Last updated: June 2026 · Itqan Business Services team

Most application delays are not due to complicated procedures but to avoidable mistakes. We have gathered the most frequent ones, with a way to avoid each.

⚠️ Regulations, conditions and requirements are constantly being renewed and may change after this article’s publication date. What appears here is general information, not legal advice, so we confirm the current requirements for your activity before any submission.

Why Do These Mistakes Repeat?

Because many investors treat the application as a form to be filled in, when it is a file read from several angles: the foreign company, the activity, the documents and compliance with sector regulations. Any weak angle stalls the whole file. The good news is that most of these mistakes can be caught by an organised review before submission.

1. An Activity That Does Not Match the Registration Type

One of the commonest mistakes is choosing an activity that falls under the commercial licence and applying for a service registration, or the reverse. The fix: first identify the registration type your activity falls under. See the difference between service and commercial licences and our article on choosing your registration activity.

2. Choosing the Activity Without Studying Restrictions and Approvals

Your activity may be open but need another authority’s approval, or fall under the excluded activities. The fix: review the activity and its regulators before preparing the file, not after.

3. Missing Financial Statements for the Foreign Company

The foreign company’s financial statements for the last fiscal year are required, and are often forgotten or not ready. The fix: prepare them, authenticated, before submission. If your company is new and has no statements for a full fiscal year, we acquire an existing, qualified foreign company for you in line with the investment registration conditions.

4. Authentication Errors

Authentication is by the Saudi embassy or by Apostille from countries where it is available. Some investors choose the route that does not suit their country, or leave authentication to the last moment. The fix: know the authentication route approved for your country and start it early.

5. Outdated Documents or Inconsistent Names

Name spellings, issue dates and register numbers must match across documents. The fix: standardise how names are written from the start and attach what explains any difference.

6. An Unclear Ownership Structure

Not stating the partners, their shares and the ownership structure clearly stalls the application. The fix: prepare a detailed ownership chart down to the final owner.

7. Misjudging Time and Budget

Some investors expect everything in days, then are surprised by the authentication stage and by the registrations after investment registration. The fix: set a realistic schedule that includes what comes after registration.

8. Neglecting the Annual Update

The Investment Law’s implementing regulations require registration details to be updated annually within the period they set, and delay may result in having to re-register. The fix: set a clear annual reminder and prepare changes in advance.

9. Relying on Ready-Made Incorporation Contract Templates

Copying another company’s contract may not fit your activity or leave gaps. The fix: drafting that suits your activity, your partners and your company’s future.

A Last Checklist Before You Press “Submit”

  • A precise activity that matches the registration type.
  • Clear sector approvals.
  • The foreign company’s commercial registration and financial statements, authenticated.
  • Matching names and details.
  • A detailed ownership structure.
  • A realistic schedule and a reminder for the annual update.
